Supplier quote decision guide

How to Compare Supplier Quotes Without Choosing on Unit Price Alone

The lowest quoted unit price is not necessarily the lowest-cost order. MOQ, freight, Incoterms, duties, other charges and lead time can change the decision before the goods reach your customer.

Quick answer

Normalize the quote before you compare it.

Start by confirming that every supplier is quoting the same product specification, order quantity and currency assumptions. Then compare unit price, MOQ, Incoterm, freight, duty or tax, other costs, lead time and effective cost per unit.

Effective Cost per Unit(Product Cost + Freight + Duty/Tax + Other Costs) / Order Quantity

This is a practical comparison estimate. Actual landed cost may require additional charges, valuation rules or shipment-specific costs.

What this shows

Supplier B has the lower estimated effective cost in this specific example, but it also has a higher MOQ and longer lead time. That does not make Supplier B automatically the better supplier. Inventory needs, cash commitment, specifications, quality and other commercial factors still matter.

The first trap

Why unit price alone can be misleading

Supplier quotations often describe different commercial packages. An EXW quote may leave more origin and export work with you; a CIF quote may include freight to a destination port; a DDP quote may include more delivery and import work. The headline price is only comparable after you understand what sits behind it.

MOQ can change the cash commitment, freight can vary by route and shipment size, and duty or tax assumptions may not match. Packaging, tooling, inspection, documentation and other charges can also sit outside the unit price. Lead time matters when a delay affects a selling season or replenishment plan.

A practical method

Five steps to compare supplier quotations

01

Make sure you are comparing the same product

Check material, dimensions, packaging, quality requirements, customization, certification and testing. A price comparison is not meaningful if the specifications are different.

02

Normalize quantity and MOQ

Separate the planned order quantity from the supplier MOQ and any price-break quantity. A lower unit price with a much larger MOQ can increase cash and inventory exposure.

Compare Incoterms and logistics costs

FAQ

Questions import teams ask

What should I compare in supplier quotations?

Compare the same product specification, unit price, order quantity, MOQ, currency, Incoterm, freight, duty or tax assumptions, other costs, lead time and effective cost per unit.

Is the supplier with the lowest unit price always cheaper?

No. Freight, duties, other charges, MOQ and different Incoterms can make a lower quoted unit price produce a higher total or effective cost.

How do I compare EXW and FOB supplier quotes?

Record what each term includes, then add the costs that remain with the buyer so both offers use a comparable cost basis. Actual responsibilities depend on the commercial terms and shipment.

How does MOQ affect supplier quote comparison?

MOQ changes the cash required and the amount of inventory committed. A lower unit price may still create more inventory exposure if it requires a much larger order.

What is effective cost per unit?

It is a practical comparison estimate calculated by adding product cost, freight, duty or tax and other costs, then dividing by the order quantity.
