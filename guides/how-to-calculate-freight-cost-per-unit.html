Freight cost guide

How to Calculate Freight Cost per Unit

Freight quotes are usually given as shipment totals. Converting that total into a per-unit cost makes supplier, margin and landed-cost comparisons much easier.

Quick answer

Add the shipment-level logistics costs, then divide by the units that should absorb them.

Freight / Logistics Cost per Unit(Origin + International Freight + Insurance + Clearance + Destination + Other Logistics Costs) / Quantity

If the shipment contains several SKUs with very different weights or volumes, a simple equal-per-unit allocation may be misleading. Use weight, volume or another reasonable driver instead.

Build the logistics stack

What costs should be included?

01

Origin movement

Pickup, inland haulage and export-side handling that is not already included in the supplier quote.

02

Main international freight

Ocean, air, rail, courier or other international transport.

03

Insurance

Include cargo insurance when it is a separate buyer-paid cost.

04

Clearance and destination logistics

Broker or clearance fees, terminal charges where applicable, inland delivery and other direct logistics costs up to the chosen destination.

Allocation method

Which quantity should absorb the freight?

For a single-SKU order, ordered quantity is a useful starting point. For commercial margin planning, expected sellable quantity can be more conservative if samples, defects or damage are expected.

For mixed-SKU shipments, allocate freight using a driver that reflects how the carrier charges: gross weight, chargeable weight, cubic volume, pallet space or declared value where appropriate.

Comparing transport modes

Use cost per unit to compare air, sea and courier quotes.

A higher shipment total may still be reasonable if it supports a smaller quantity, shorter lead time or lower inventory exposure. Compare freight per unit together with lead time and the cash tied up in inventory.
