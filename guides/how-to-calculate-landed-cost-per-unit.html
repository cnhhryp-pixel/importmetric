Quick answer

Calculate the complete order cost, then divide by quantity.

Define the destination first, then add every direct cost required to get the goods there. ImportMetric V1 treats this as a planning estimate, not a universal customs rule.

Total Landed CostProduct Cost + Origin / Inland Transport + International Freight + Insurance + Import Duty + Customs / Clearance + Destination Delivery + Other Import Costs
Landed Cost per UnitTotal Landed Cost / Order Quantity

Duty and tax treatment varies by product and destination. Verify actual customs treatment before committing to an order.

Read the result correctly

Supplier price vs landed cost

The supplier price in this example is $8.40 per unit. The estimated landed cost is $10.29 per unit. The difference is approximately $1.89 per unit, representing the non-product costs in this scenario.

That gap is useful for comparing supplier economics, planning margin and reviewing the purchase order. It is still an estimate: actual charges, classification, destination rules and commercial terms can change the final result.

Build the cost stack

What costs belong in landed cost?

01

Product / purchase cost

The supplier invoice value for the goods, including directly attributable packaging, tooling or setup charges where appropriate.

02

Origin transport

Inland movement and origin handling needed to move goods from the supplier to the export or shipping point.

03

International freight and insurance

The main transport cost and the insurance cost used to protect the shipment while it is moving.

04

Duty and customs

Import duty, customs clearance, broker or documentation fees and other border charges. The basis and treatment vary by jurisdiction.

05

Destination delivery and other direct import costs

Delivery to the defined destination plus inspection, storage before receipt or other direct import charges. Selling-channel costs such as advertising, marketplace commissions and customer acquisition are normally evaluated separately in selling economics.

A practical method

Five steps to a usable landed cost

01

Define the destination of your calculation

“Landed” needs a defined destination: port, warehouse, fulfillment center or another handoff point. Include costs consistently up to that point.

02

Start with the complete supplier cost

Multiply unit price by quantity and include directly attributable packaging, tooling, setup and supplier-side charges. An incomplete purchase cost creates a misleading result.

03

Add freight and insurance

Include origin transport, international freight, insurance and destination transport. Freight quotes may not contain every destination or handling charge.

04

Estimate duty and customs costs carefully

Duty basis and tax treatment vary by jurisdiction, product classification and destination. ImportMetric V1 uses a clearly disclosed planning assumption; verify actual treatment with the relevant customs authority, broker or qualified professional.

05

Divide the complete order cost by quantity

$10,285.53 / 1,000 = $10.28553, approximately $10.29 per unit. This per-unit number helps compare supplier economics, set prices, plan margin and review purchase decisions.

Common mistakes

Where landed cost calculations go wrong

Common errors include treating supplier unit price as total cost, forgetting destination or local charges, comparing different Incoterms as if they were equivalent, using an unverified duty assumption, forgetting fixed clearance charges, dividing by the wrong quantity, or mixing marketplace and selling costs into import costs without a clear model.

FAQ

Questions import teams ask

What is landed cost per unit?

Landed cost per unit is the total estimated cost of an imported order, including product and direct import costs, divided by the order quantity.

What costs should be included in landed cost?

Include product cost, origin transport, international freight, insurance, import duty, customs or clearance fees, destination delivery and other direct import costs.

Is freight included in landed cost?

Yes. International freight and relevant origin or destination transport should be included when they are direct costs required to move the goods to the defined destination.

How do Incoterms affect landed cost?

Incoterms describe how commercial responsibilities and costs are commonly allocated, so quotes using different terms may require different additional cost estimates before comparison.

Is landed cost the same as product cost?

No. Product cost is the supplier purchase amount. Landed cost adds the direct freight, insurance, duty, customs, delivery and other import costs needed to reach the defined destination.
