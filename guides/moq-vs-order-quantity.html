MOQ vs Order Quantity: How Much Should You Actually Buy?

A supplier's minimum order quantity is a commercial constraint. Your ideal order quantity is a demand and cash-flow decision. They are related, but they are not the same number.

Quick answer

MOQ is the supplier's floor. Order quantity should come from your demand plan.

If a supplier requires 3,000 units but your target inventory coverage only supports 1,300 units, the extra 1,700 units are not automatically “cheap inventory.” They are additional cash, storage and sell-through risk.

What the example reveals

The supplier minimum creates more than twice the target purchase quantity. The question is no longer “Is $4.50 a good price?” It becomes “Can the business absorb an extra $7,650 of inventory risk?”

Two different numbers

Why MOQ and ideal order quantity diverge.

01

MOQ is driven by the supplier

Production setup, packaging runs, material purchasing and commercial policy can all influence the supplier minimum.

02

Order quantity is driven by your business

Your quantity should reflect sell-through, working capital, lead time, safety stock, storage and how quickly a product may become obsolete.

03

A lower unit price can increase total risk

A supplier may offer a lower price at a higher MOQ. The price saving should be compared against the extra cash locked in inventory and the probability of slow sell-through.

Decision framework

Five questions before accepting an MOQ.

01

What quantity does demand support?

Estimate a conservative monthly sales rate and choose a target months-of-cover range.

02

What happens during lead time?

Long replenishment lead times may justify more inventory, but they should be modeled rather than assumed.

03

How much cash becomes unavailable?

Inventory competes with marketing, new products, freight and operating expenses for the same cash.

04

Can the MOQ be negotiated?

Options include mixed colors, shared packaging, staged releases, higher first-order pricing or a smaller trial run.

05

What is the downside if sales are slower?

Run a second scenario at a lower sales rate. If inventory coverage becomes uncomfortable, the original order is fragile.

Common mistakes

What buyers often miss.

Common errors include treating MOQ as the recommended quantity, using optimistic sales forecasts, ignoring existing inventory, focusing only on price breaks, and forgetting storage or obsolescence risk.
