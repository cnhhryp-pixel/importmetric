The sourcing math

Work backwards
from margin.

To protect your target profit margin, start with what the customer pays and subtract the profit you need. What remains is the maximum landed cost per unit. Subtract your non-product costs to find the highest supplier price you can accept.

Maximum supplier price / unitTarget selling price − target profit − non-product costs / unit
Target profit / unitTarget selling price × target profit margin %

This is a planning estimate. Confirm taxes, platform fees, fulfillment charges and import costs against your actual commercial terms before placing an order.

Practical sourcing questions

Before you
negotiate.

Use the price ceiling as a decision boundary, not as a substitute for reviewing the complete supplier quote.

What does maximum purchase price mean?

It is the highest supplier unit price you can pay while still reaching your target profit margin after the costs entered in the calculator.

Should I negotiate below this price?

Usually, yes. The result is a ceiling based on your assumptions. A lower supplier price creates room for cost uncertainty, discounts, returns, exchange-rate movement or a stronger margin.

What if my non-product costs change?

Update freight, duty, marketplace fees, customs and fulfillment costs before comparing offers. Every additional cost reduces the supplier price you can afford by the same amount per unit.
